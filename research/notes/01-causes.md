# 01 — What is actually causing low attention span?

## First, kill the myth
The "human attention span is now 8 seconds, shorter than a goldfish" claim is **not real
science.** It traces to a 2015 Microsoft Canada marketing report whose footnote cited
"Statistic Brain," which could produce no credible source; the BBC (2017) found the data
fabricated. Goldfish don't have 9-second memories either. **We should never ship this stat.**

But debunking the myth is not the same as saying nothing is wrong. Something *is* wrong —
just not what the meme says.

## What the real data shows

**1. Sustained on-screen attention has genuinely shrunk.**
Gloria Mark's lab (UC Irvine), measuring real people at real computers, found average
sustained attention on a single screen dropped from **~2.5 minutes (mid-2000s) to ~47
seconds today** (*Attention Span*, 2023). This is direct observation, not a survey.

**2. Interruptions are catastrophically expensive to recover from.**
- After an interruption it takes on average **~23 minutes** to return to the original task
  at the same depth of focus (Mark's field research).
- People typically do **two other tasks** before circling back to the interrupted one.
- **Attention residue** (Leroy, 2009): even after you switch, part of your mind stays stuck
  on the previous task, degrading the next one for its whole duration.

The implication is stark: a person interrupted every ~10 minutes may **never** reach deep
focus. The cost isn't the 10 seconds of the notification — it's the 23-minute crater it leaves.

**3. The environment changed, not human biology.**
The causal story isn't "brains got worse." It's that the information environment now
delivers constant, unpredictable, self-reinforcing interruptions (notifications, feeds,
autoplay) engineered to capture attention. See note 02 for the machinery.

## The behavioral pattern (the user's own description)
- Compulsive app-browsing / "just checking"
- Doomscrolling (note 03)
- Long binge sessions (shows, feeds) — time blindness
- Difficulty returning to and sustaining a single task

These are the *symptoms*. The causes below feed them.

## Causal contributors (synthesis)
| Contributor | Mechanism | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Variable-reward feeds | Slot-machine reinforcement schedule | note 02 |
| Notification interruption | 23-min refocus cost + attention residue | Mark 2008; Leroy 2009 |
| Short-form video conditioning | Trains brain to expect rapid high-reward stimuli | note 03 |
| Algorithmic infinite scroll | Removes natural stopping cues | doomscrolling review |
| Pre-existing distractibility/ADHD | Selection effect — draws vulnerable users in | note 03 |

## Product implication
The lever is **the interruption and the loop**, not "screen time" as a number. Protecting
unbroken blocks of attention (because of the 23-min refocus cost) and breaking the
variable-reward cycle are the two highest-value targets.
