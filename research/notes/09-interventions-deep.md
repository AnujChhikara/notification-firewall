# 09 — What actually works (deep, evidence-graded)

Extends note 04 with the mechanistic evidence. Graded by strength of evidence.

## TIER 1 — Strong, directly product-relevant

### Friction / self-nudge at the point of temptation ⭐ (the keystone)
**Grüning et al. (2023), PNAS — the One Sec RCT** (downloaded). 280 users, 6 weeks, in-the-wild.
- When One Sec interposed a breath + short delay + "do you want to continue?" before a target
  app, users **abandoned the app 36% of the time**.
- App-open attempts dropped **~37%** from week 1 to week 6 — i.e., it **did not fully fade**.
- **The single most effective element was offering the _option to dismiss_** (the deliberate
  choice), with the time-delay friction second.
- Users overwhelmingly targeted **social media**.

**Why it works (ties the whole KB together):** it's an **implementation intention** (note 06)
+ **added friction on the bad routine** (note 07) + **breaking the Hook's frictionless Action
step** (note 08), preserving **autonomy** via the dismiss option (note 06). This is the most
theory-consistent, best-evidenced single mechanism we have. **Build around this.**

### Environmental design that persists (note 04, recap)
Notification management (kill non-human notifications), grayscale, phone out of the bedroom
(highest-yield for sleep), deleting/relocating culprit apps + re-entry friction, reduced-use
protocols. **"Design changes persist where willpower doesn't."**

## TIER 2 — Real capacity-building (indirect, but genuine)

### Sleep — protect it, because it's upstream of all attention
Lim & Dinges (downloaded) + recent Nature Neuroscience: sleep deprivation **directly causes
attention lapses** — slower reactions, more omissions, failures of vigilant attention. New
work links lapses to CSF-flow dynamics. Sleep isn't adjacent to attention; it's the substrate.
→ The strongest *health* lever the app can pull is protecting the last waking hour + sleep.
(This is also why the existing product already guards a Sleep profile — keep that.)

### Aerobic exercise — reliably boosts executive function/attention
Meta-analyses: acute aerobic exercise gives an **immediate** boost to inhibitory control,
working memory, and attention; benefits are **largest for those starting lower** and for
ADHD populations. Cheap, fast-acting, real. → A credible "restorative substitute" to offer
instead of a scroll.

### Mindfulness meditation — trains executive attention
Tang & Posner (2007, PNAS): **5 days × 20 min** improved executive attention & self-regulation.
Meditation recruits exactly the executive-attention network (ACC, insula, striatum — note 05).
Meta-analyses show modest but real gains in executive control; some evidence it *preserves*
sustained attention and healthy default-mode/DLPFC anticorrelation. → A legitimate,
mechanism-backed "attention gym," best in small daily doses.

## TIER 3 — Restoration
Attention Restoration Theory (note 04/05): low-demand/natural environments **replenish**
depleted directed attention. A real break restores; another feed does not.

## What does NOT work (avoid building these)

### ❌ "Brain-training" games (far transfer basically doesn't happen)
Melby-Lervåg, Redick & Hulme (2016) + Simons et al. (2016): working-memory / cognitive
training produces **near transfer only** — you get better at the game, **not** at general
attention, intelligence, or real-world focus. **Do not build attention mini-games and claim
they fix attention.** The evidence is squarely against it.

### ❌ "Dopamine detox" as a frame
Pop-science; you can't fast dopamine (notes 02, 04). To the extent it helps it's just
reduced exposure to hyper-stimuli — an environmental change. Don't brand around it.

### ⚠️ Willpower / gamified streaks / hard lockouts (fade fast)
Reviews: most focus apps **work ~1 week, ineffective by ~3 weeks** — Forest-style
gamification fades fastest once novelty dies; friction (One Sec) and scheduled blocking
(Opal) last slightly longer. Combined with **reactance** from hard blocks (note 06) and
Lally's finding that habits aren't even formed by 3 weeks (note 07), this is the **central
retention problem to solve.**

## The evidence-backed design stack (what to actually build)
1. **Friction + deliberate-choice prompt** at the moment of opening a target app (One Sec–style),
   authored by the user as if-then rules, **with an override** (autonomy).
2. **Environmental defaults**: strip external triggers/notifications; protect sleep window.
3. **Offer a restorative substitute** at the moment of temptation (breathe, move, step away)
   rather than only blocking.
4. **Forgive lapses; design for a 60–250-day horizon** to beat the 3-week cliff.
5. **Do NOT** promise brain-training gains or dopamine "resets."
