# 08 — The attention economy: what we're up against

Know the enemy's playbook precisely — the product is a *counter-design* to it.

## The business model IS the problem
In the **attention economy**, platforms monetize engagement (ad revenue ∝ time & attention).
Their objective function is **maximize time-on-app**, which is *directly opposed* to the
user's attention and wellbeing (Tristan Harris, Center for Humane Technology; ex-Google
design ethicist). This is not incidental — the harm is the KPI. Our product's KPI must be
the *inverse*: attention returned to the user.

## The core weapon: intermittent variable rewards (Skinner)
The most powerful reinforcement schedule known (B.F. Skinner, 1950s): tie an action to a
reward that arrives **unpredictably**. Addictiveness is **maximized when reward rate is most
variable** — i.e., a slot machine. Applied everywhere:
- Pull-to-refresh = pulling the lever.
- Likes/comments/notifications = unpredictable payouts.
- Algorithmic feed = a personalized variable-reward optimizer.

The *anticipation* of the maybe-reward drives compulsion more than the reward (note 02).

## The Hook Model (Nir Eyal, *Hooked*)
The explicit blueprint used to build habit-forming products — a 4-step loop designers run
to manufacture compulsion:
1. **Trigger** — external (notification) then internal (boredom, loneliness, anxiety).
2. **Action** — the simplest behavior in anticipation of reward (scroll, tap).
3. **Variable Reward** — the unpredictable payoff (rewards of the tribe/hunt/self).
4. **Investment** — user puts in time/data/content, which loads the *next* trigger and
   raises switching cost.

**Every step is a design lever we can reverse:**
| Hook step | Their move | Our counter-move |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Maximize prompts; hook internal triggers | Strip external triggers; surface the internal trigger to the user |
| Action | Minimize friction to act | *Add* friction at the moment of the compulsive action |
| Variable reward | Maximize unpredictability | Make the reward predictable/boring; break anticipation |
| Investment | Increase sunk cost & switching cost | Lower the cost of *leaving*; make exit frictionless |

## Fogg's Persuasive Technology Lab (Stanford) — the academic root
The B=MAP model (note 07) was developed in Stanford's **Persuasive Technology Lab**, which
trained a generation of designers (including Eyal and, adjacently, Instagram founders). The
*same* behavior science can be used **for** the user (a self-nudge toward their goals) or
**against** them (engagement maximization). We are choosing the pro-user direction of the
identical toolkit.

## Persuasive/"dark pattern" techniques catalogued
(Vivrekar 2018, Stanford thesis — downloaded; + arXiv Chinese-students study)
Infinite scroll, autoplay, badges/counts, streaks & loss-aversion, social reciprocity
pressure ("seen"/typing indicators), pull-to-refresh, red-dot notification bait, algorithmic
recommendation. Each removes a natural **stopping cue** or manufactures a trigger.

## The strategic thesis for the product
1. **We are the anti-Hook.** For each of the four Hook steps, do the opposite.
2. **Reintroduce stopping cues** the platforms deliberately removed (ends, pauses, limits).
3. **Externalize the internal trigger:** help users *notice* "I'm opening this because I'm
   bored/anxious," which is the moment behavior change becomes possible.
4. **Our success metric is the platforms' failure metric** — attention *not* captured.
5. This is genuinely *humane technology* (per Harris/CHT): behavior design realigned to the
   user's own stated goals, with autonomy preserved (note 06).
