# 04 — What actually works to rebuild attention

The single most important finding for this product:

> **Design changes persist where willpower doesn't.**

Structural/environmental changes that alter *what is easy* outperform motivation-based
abstinence. This is the empirical backbone of a firewall-style product.

## What has weak / inconsistent evidence
- **Total "digital detox" / abstinence.** Systematic reviews show mixed results: some mood
  improvement, a comparable number show no change, and several document **negative effects**
  (boredom, anxiety) *during* the detox. Willpower-based abstinence doesn't stick.
- **"Dopamine detox."** Pop-science oversimplification; you can't fast dopamine. To the
  extent it helps, it's really a *targeted reduction of hyper-stimulating content* — i.e. an
  environmental change wearing a neuroscience costume. Don't build or brand around it.

## What has better evidence
**Time-limited social-media breaks (structured, not total).**
University of Bath RCT (2022): heavy users assigned **one week off social platforms** showed
significant improvements in depression, anxiety, and wellbeing vs controls. **But** effects
are modest and **fade within weeks without structural change** → one-off breaks aren't enough;
the environment has to change durably.

**Environmental / friction interventions that persist:**
| Intervention | Why it works | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Notification management** (disable non-human notifications, batch the rest) | Removes externally-triggered anticipation; cuts interruptions | Simply Psychology synthesis; Mark (interruption cost) |
| **Grayscale display** | Strips the reward salience of color; adds friction | Detox-trends evidence |
| **Phone out of the bedroom** | "Single highest-yield change" for sleep, which guards next-day attention | Simply Psychology synthesis |
| **Delete/relocate culprit apps + re-entry friction** (password, extra taps) | Converts autopilot into a deliberate decision | Detox evidence |
| **Reduced-use protocols** (limits, not zero) | Improves attention + wellbeing *without* total abstinence | Detox evidence |
| **Introduce friction at the moment of opening** | Interrupts the anticipation→check reflex before it completes | (see gap: One Sec RCT) |

**Attention Restoration Theory (Kaplan & Kaplan, 1989).**
Directed attention is a depletable resource; **natural / low-stimulation environments
replenish it.** Restorative breaks (nature, paper, low-stim) rebuild capacity better than
switching to *another* stimulating screen. Implication: a good "break" is not another feed.

**Substitution over suppression.**
Replacing screens with paper (journaling, planning) improves retention, attention, and
creativity. Giving the freed attention somewhere to go beats leaving a vacuum.

## Design principles distilled (for the rebrand)
1. **Change the environment, not the person.** Reduce friction toward focus; add friction
   toward the loop. Never rely on the user "wanting it enough."
2. **Diagnose, then target.** Detoxes work best as *diagnostic tools* — find which apps and
   moments create problems, then intervene *there* precisely, not everywhere.
3. **Friction at the moment of temptation** (opening the app, the anticipation beat) is
   cheap and high-leverage.
4. **Protect blocks, don't count minutes.** Given the 23-min refocus cost, unbroken time
   matters more than total screen time.
5. **Offer a landing spot.** Pair suppression with a restorative or substitute action.
6. **Durable structure > one-time breaks.** Effects fade without persistent change — build
   for standing structure, not heroic sprints.
7. **Avoid the "detox/willpower" narrative** — the evidence undercuts it and it sets users
   up to fail.

## Open questions to resolve before design
- Friction-app RCT evidence (One Sec / Grüning et al.) — directly validates the friction thesis.
- What's the right unit: block-protection, app-gating, feed-neutralization, or all three?
- How to make interventions *stick* past the fade-out window the RCTs warn about.
