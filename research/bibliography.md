## A. Is attention span really declining? (the myth + the real data)

**Mark, G. (2023). _Attention Span: A Groundbreaking Way to Restore Balance, Happiness and Productivity._ Hanover Square Press.** 📄🔒
Reports her lab's measurement that average sustained attention on a screen fell from
~2.5 minutes (mid-2000s) to ~**47 seconds** today. The most credible empirical anchor
for "attention is shrinking."

**Mark, G., Gudith, D., & Klocke, U. (2008). The Cost of Interrupted Work: More Speed and Stress. _CHI 2008._** 📄✅
`sources/papers/mark2008-cost-of-interrupted-work.pdf` — Classic study. Interrupted work
is completed *faster* but with more stress, frustration, and effort. Foundation for the
"interruptions are costly" thesis. (The widely quoted "**23 min 15 s to refocus**" figure
comes from Mark's related field work.)

**Leroy, S. (2009). Why is it so hard to do my work? The challenge of attention residue. _Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes._** 📄🔒
Coined **"attention residue"** — cognitive activity about a prior task persists into the
next, degrading performance for the whole session. Explains why task-switching is corrosive.

**The 8-second / goldfish myth — DEBUNKED.** 🌐📰
The "human attention span dropped to 8 seconds, less than a goldfish" claim (Microsoft
Canada, 2015) traces to a footnote citing "Statistic Brain," which had **no credible
source**; the BBC (2017) found the underlying data fabricated. Goldfish also don't have a
9-second memory. → *Do not use this stat in product copy.*
Sources: netpsychology.org, medium.com/psynamic, BBC investigation.

## B. Mechanisms — dopamine & reward loops

**Westbrook, A., Ghosh, A., van den Bosch, R., Määttä, J.I., Hofmans, L., & Cools, R. (2021). Striatal dopamine synthesis capacity reflects smartphone social activity. _iScience_, 24(5), 102497.** 📄🌐
[PMC8170001](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8170001/) — n=22 adults, [18F]-DOPA PET
+ passive logging (~32 days). A higher proportion of **social** app use correlated with
**lower** dopamine synthesis capacity in the putamen (β=−1.5×10⁻³, p=1.3×10⁻⁴; 55% of
variance). **Important nuance:** the naive "apps flood you with dopamine" story is too
simple — heavy social use tracks with an *altered* dopamine system, and the effect is
selective to *social* use, not screen time generally.

**Variable-ratio reinforcement / reward-prediction-error framing.** 📰🌐
Well-established secondary synthesis: likes/notifications are **unpredictable rewards** on a
variable-ratio schedule (slot-machine math) that maximize dopamine release in the ventral
striatum; the *anticipation* phase drives compulsive checking more than the reward itself.
Sources: netpsychology.org, neurosity.co, neurolaunch.com. (Use as framing, cite primary
neuroscience before making strong claims.)

## C. Short-form video & cognition

**[Systematic review] The Impact of Short-Form Video Use on Cognitive and Mental Health Outcomes. (2025). _medRxiv_ 2025.08.27.25334540.** 📄🔒(PDF JS-gated)
[medRxiv link](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.27.25334540v1.full) — Systematic
review tying heavy short-form video use to attentional disruption, reduced executive
function, emotional dysregulation, and compulsive use.

**Impact of Short Reels on Attention Span and Academic Performance. _ERIC_ EJ1454296.** 📄✅
`sources/papers/short-reels-attention-academic.pdf` — 1–2 h/day of short-form video
associated with worse study concentration; negative association with study habits.

**Shashua, A. TikTok Brain and Cognitive Costs: Attention, Learning, and Short-Form Video Culture. _SSRN_ 5399417.** 📄🔒
"TikTok brain": rapid-scroll conditioning trains the brain to expect quick high-reward
stimuli, favoring brief bursts over sustained concentration.

**Short-form Video Use and Sustained Attention: A Narrative Review (2019–2025). _ResearchGate_ 397712802.** 📄🔒
Narrative synthesis; greater TikTok use → poorer sustained attention + adverse academic
performance.

**"Attention in the Age of TikTok." SJSU Master's Thesis.** 📄🌐 (scholarworks.sjsu.edu)

## D. ADHD & digital media

**Thorell, L.B., Burén, J., Ström Wiman, J., Sandberg, D., & Nutley, S.B. (2022). Longitudinal associations between digital media use and ADHD symptoms in children and adolescents: a systematic literature review. _European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry_, 33(8), 2503–2526.** 📄🌐
[PMC11272698](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272698/) — 28 longitudinal studies
(2011–2021). ~74% found significant associations, but **effect sizes small** (r<0.30).
**Reciprocal:** media→ADHD (63% of studies) *and* ADHD→media (53%). Associations stronger
for **problematic use** than raw screen time, and weaken when controlling for baseline ADHD.

**Ra, C.K., Cho, J., Stone, M.D., et al. (2018). Association of Digital Media Use With Subsequent Symptoms of ADHD Among Adolescents. _JAMA_, 320(3), 255–263.** 📄🌐
[JAMA fulltext](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2687861) — n=2,587 teens,
no baseline ADHD, 24-mo follow-up. Each additional high-frequency digital activity → OR
**1.10** (95% CI 1.05–1.15) for later ADHD symptoms. **Dose-response**: 4.6% symptom
prevalence at 0 activities vs 9.5% at 7. Landmark longitudinal evidence.

## E. Doomscrolling

**Sharpe, A.T.R., Tyndall, I., Poulus, D.R., Obine, E.A., & Sharpe, B.T. (2026). The influence of doomscrolling on mental health: a scoping review. _Mental Health and Digital Technologies_, 3(3), 290–342.** 📄🌐
[Emerald link](https://www.emerald.com/mhdt/article/3/3/290/1338079/) — 17 empirical studies
(PRISMA-ScR). Compulsive negative-content consumption → anxiety, depression, stress,
diminished resilience. Driven by **infinite scroll + algorithmic delivery** and sustained
by rumination + intolerance of uncertainty. Evidence still mostly cross-sectional.

**UF study: "doomscrolling is a unique behavior" (2022).** 📰🌐 (news.ufl.edu)

## F. Interventions — what actually works

**[RCT] Frontiers in Human Dynamics (2025). Digital detox as a means to enhance eudaimonic well-being. 10.3389/fhumd.2025.1572587.** 📄✅
`sources/papers/frontiers2025-digital-detox-eudaimonic.pdf`

**Lambert, J., et al. / University of Bath (2022) — one-week social-media break RCT.** 📄🌐
Heavy users assigned one week off social platforms showed significant improvements in
depression, anxiety, and wellbeing vs controls. Effects **modest and fade** without
structural change. (Via Simply Psychology evidence review.)

**Simply Psychology — "Do Digital Detoxes Actually Work?" (2026).** 🌐📰
[link](https://www.simplypsychology.com/articles/digital-detox-evidence) — Synthesis: total
abstinence = weak/inconsistent; **targeted structural changes persist**: notification
management (disable non-human notifications), grayscale, phone out of bedroom (highest-yield
for sleep), deleting specific culprit apps + friction (password re-entry), reduced-use
protocols. Thesis line: **"design changes persist where willpower doesn't."**

**Kaplan, S. & Kaplan, R. (1989). Attention Restoration Theory (ART).** 📄🔒
Natural environments replenish depleted directed-attention resources. Basis for
"restorative" breaks vs more stimulation.

# PART 2 — Foundational science of attention & behavior change (deep dive)

## G. How attention works (cognitive neuroscience)

**Posner, M.I. & Petersen, S.E. (1990) + Petersen & Posner (2012). The attention system of the human brain: 20 years after. _Annual Review of Neuroscience_, 35, 73–89.** 📄🔒
The three-network model: **alerting, orienting, executive control.** Foundational. Orienting
splits into bottom-up (exogenous capture) vs top-down (endogenous control) — the core
battleground for distraction. → note 05.

**Diamond, A. (2013). Executive Functions. _Annual Review of Psychology_, 64, 135–168.** 📄✅
`sources/papers/diamond2013-executive-functions.pdf` — The canonical EF review. Three cores:
**inhibitory control, working memory, cognitive flexibility.** Inhibitory control is what the
attention economy attacks. → note 05.

**Miyake, A., et al. (2000) / Miyake & Friedman (2012). The unity and diversity of executive functions.** 📄🔒
Factor-analytic basis for the three separable-but-correlated EF components. → note 05.

**Csikszentmihalyi, M. — Flow.** 📄🔒 (book/theory)
Complete absorption in one task when challenge≈skill; requires clear goals, feedback, and
**freedom from interruption.** The positive target state the product should protect. → note 05.

## H. Self-regulation, willpower, motivation

**Hagger, M.S., et al. (2016). A multilab preregistered replication of the ego-depletion effect. _Perspectives on Psychological Science_, 11(4).** 📄🔒
23-lab preregistered replication → **~no ego-depletion effect.** Willpower-as-fuel is
unreliable. (Contrast Hagger 2010 meta, d≈0.62, which failed to replicate.) → note 06.

**Ego depletion — reinterpretation as motivation/attention shift.** 🌐📄 (Wikipedia + ScienceDirect 2024 status review)
"Depletion" better explained as a **shift in motivation and attention** toward reward, not a
depleted resource. → note 06.

**Gollwitzer, P.M. & Sheeran, P. (2006). Implementation Intentions and Goal Achievement: A Meta-analysis of Effects and Processes. _Advances in Experimental Social Psychology_, 38, 69–119.** 📄✅
`sources/papers/gollwitzer-implementation-intentions.pdf` — **If-then plans**, 94 tests, **d≈0.65**.
Delegates control to environmental cues → automatic goal-directed response, bypassing
in-the-moment willpower. 2024 update (Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer): 642 tests. **Core
mechanism for the product.** → note 06.

**Deci, E.L. & Ryan, R.M. — Self-Determination Theory.** 📄🔒
Durable motivation needs **autonomy, competence, relatedness.** Hard blocking undermines
autonomy → reactance → uninstall. Nudge > jail. → note 06.

## I. Habit formation & behavior-change models

**Lally, P., et al. (2010). How are habits formed: Modelling habit formation in the real world. _European Journal of Social Psychology_, 40(6), 998–1009.** 📄🔒
96 people; median time to automaticity **66 days** (range 18–254). **"21 days" is a myth.**
Missing one day didn't derail formation → **forgive lapses.** → note 07.

**Fogg, B.J. (2009 / _Tiny Habits_ 2019). Fogg Behavior Model: B = MAP.** 📄🌐 (behaviormodel.org)
Behavior = **Motivation × Ability × Prompt** converging. Key: **raising Ability (ease) beats
raising Motivation.** Stanford Persuasive Tech Lab origin. → notes 07, 08.

**Michie, S., van Stralen, M.M., & West, R. (2011). The behaviour change wheel / COM-B. _Implementation Science_, 6:42.** 📄🌐
Behaviour = **Capability + Opportunity + Motivation.** Environment (Opportunity) is the most
app-designable, highest-leverage point. → note 07.

**Thaler, R. & Sunstein, C. — Nudge / choice architecture.** 📄🔒
Friction, defaults, and presentation change behavior without removing options. Basis of the
"self-nudge." → notes 07, 09.

## J. The attention economy / persuasive tech (the adversary)

**Harris, T. — Center for Humane Technology; "How Technology Hijacks People's Minds" (2016).** 🌐📰
Attention economy: platforms maximize engagement against user wellbeing; intermittent
variable rewards (Skinner) as the core weapon. → note 08.

**Eyal, N. — _Hooked: How to Build Habit-Forming Products._** 📄🔒
The 4-step Hook Model: **Trigger → Action → Variable Reward → Investment.** The explicit
blueprint we reverse step-by-step. → note 08.

**Vivrekar, D. (2018). Persuasive Design Techniques in the Attention Economy. Stanford Master's Thesis.** 📄✅
`sources/papers/vivrekar2018-persuasive-design-attention-economy.pdf` — Catalogue of
persuasive/dark-pattern techniques (infinite scroll, autoplay, badges, streaks, pull-to-refresh). → note 08.

**Skinner, B.F. — intermittent variable-ratio reinforcement.** 📄🔒 (foundational)
Addictiveness maximized when reward rate is most variable. The slot-machine mechanism. → notes 02, 08.

## K. Interventions — deep evidence

**Grüning, D.J., et al. (2023). Directing smartphone use through the self-nudge app one sec. _PNAS_, 120(8), e2213114120.** 📄✅ ⭐
`sources/papers/gruning2023-onesec-pnas.pdf` — **The keystone RCT.** 280 users, 6 wks.
Friction+breath+"continue?" → **36% abandon**, **~37% fewer opens** over 6 wks (didn't fully
fade). Most effective element = **the option to dismiss** (deliberate choice). → notes 06, 09.

**Lim, J. & Dinges, D.F. (2008). Sleep deprivation and vigilant attention. _Annals of the NY Academy of Sciences_, 1129, 305–322.** 📄✅
`sources/papers/lim-dinges-sleep-vigilant-attention.pdf` — Sleep loss directly causes
attention lapses (PVT). Sleep is the substrate of attention. → note 09.

**Attentional failures after sleep deprivation ... CSF flow dynamics. _Nature Neuroscience_ (2025), s41593-025-02098-8.** 📄🔒
Attention lapses coincide with CSF-flow pulses during sleep deprivation. → note 09.

**Tang, Y.-Y., Ma, Y., ... Posner, M.I. (2007). Short-term meditation training improves attention and self-regulation. _PNAS_, 104(43), 17152–17156.** 📄🔒
**5 days × 20 min** → improved executive attention & self-regulation. Recruits ACC/insula/striatum. → note 09.

**Meta-analysis: Does mindfulness meditation training enhance executive control? _Mindfulness_ (2019), 10.1007/s12671-019-01279-4.** 📄🔒
Modest but real gains in executive control across RCTs. → note 09.

**Aerobic exercise & executive function — multiple meta-analyses** (e.g., Frontiers Psychology 2024; PMC6443849 acute-exercise-ADHD). 📄🌐
Acute aerobic exercise → immediate boost to inhibitory control/WM/attention; largest for
lower-baseline & ADHD groups. → note 09.

**Melby-Lervåg, M., Redick, T.S., & Hulme, C. (2016). Working memory training does not improve ... "far transfer." _Perspectives on Psychological Science_, 11(4).** 📄🔒
**+ Simons et al. (2016).** Brain-training = **near transfer only.** No real-world attention
gains. → *Do NOT build attention mini-games and claim they fix attention.* → note 09.

## L. Existing products (competitive landscape)
Reviews of **One Sec, Opal, Forest, Freedom, Apple Screen Time / Digital Wellbeing.** 🌐📰
Consensus: friction (One Sec) + scheduled blocking (Opal) most durable; **gamified streaks
(Forest) fade fastest**; awareness dashboards weak; **all hit a ~3-week cliff.** Winning combo
= **awareness + friction + replacement**, which no single app does well. → note 10.

# PART 3 — Gap-fill: binge-watching, durability, clinical approaches, primary neuroscience

## M. Binge-watching & autoplay

**An Experimental Study of Netflix Use and the Effects of Autoplay on Watching Behaviors. (2025). _Proc. ACM Human-Computer Interaction_ / arXiv 2412.16040.** 📄✅
`sources/papers/netflix-autoplay-experiment-2025.pdf` — Disabling autoplay **reduced daily
time spent and session length.** Autoplay reverses the default (must choose to *stop*),
removing the stopping cue. → note 11.

**"Switching off": diary study on Netflix auto-play, binge-watching & mindful attention (IADT, Figshare).** 📄🌐
Autoplay ↔ more bingeing, lower mindful attention awareness. → note 11.

**Predictors of problematic binge-watching (PsyPost/2022 summary of empirical work).** 🌐📰
Impulsivity + escape/mood-regulation motive drive marathon viewing. → notes 11, 13.

## N. Durability / retention (the make-or-break)

**Nahum-Shani, I., et al. (2018). Just-in-Time Adaptive Interventions (JITAIs) in Mobile Health: Key Components and Design Principles. _Annals of Behavioral Medicine_, 52(6), 446–462.** (+ 2014 organizing-framework preprint) 📄✅ ⭐
`sources/papers/nahum-shani2014-jitai-framework.pdf` — **The durability mechanism.** Deliver
the right support at the right moment by adapting to changing internal + contextual state;
states of receptivity/vulnerability; decision points + tailoring variables + decision rules.
Relevance beats novelty. → note 12.

**mHealth engagement/attrition literature** (JMIR 2022 review PMC9092233; app-attrition
meta PMC7556375; 10,000 Steps PMC4526999). 📄🌐
**~71% disengage within 90 days; up to 98% brief use; ~2% sustained; gamification/reminders
NOT consistently effective.** Key reframe: **"frequent engagement with the intervention may
not be required to support sustained engagement with the _behavior_"** — app retention ≠
behavior change. → note 12.

## O. Clinical approaches (CBT / ACT / adult ADHD)

**Effectiveness of Psychological Treatments for Problematic Use of Internet, Video Games, Social Media & IM: Systematic Review & Meta-Analysis. (2025). _IJERPH_ (MDPI) 22(10), 1598.** 📄🔒(PDF JS-gated)
CBT most-studied; significant reductions, **pooled SMD ≈ −1.53.** STICA manualized program
(psychoeducation → behavioral analysis + exposure → relapse prevention). → note 13.

**Kantar, A., et al. (2025). Joy of Missing Out (JOMO) and Its Role in Reducing Social Media Addiction: serial mediation via loneliness & psychological distress. _Journal of Individual Differences / Sage._** 📄🔒
Higher JOMO → lower social-media addiction, mediated by lower loneliness & distress. ACT/
values-based framing (FOMO→JOMO). → note 13.

**_attexis_: a Digital CBT Intervention for Adults with ADHD — RCT. (2025). _Psychological Medicine_ (Cambridge) / medRxiv 2025.08.27.25334574.** 📄🔒(PDF JS-gated)
Digital CBT for adult ADHD; promise for inattention/impulsivity/EF, **strongest with high
adherence; evidence quality still low.** → note 13.

**Mindfulness-based interventions for adults with ADHD: systematic review & meta-analysis. _PMC12440486._** 📄🌐 → note 13.

**Systematic review of reviews: digital interventions for ADHD (effectiveness & safety). _BMC Psychiatry_ (2025), 10.1186/s12888-025-06825-0.** 📄🌐 → note 13.

## P. Primary reward neuroscience

**Hollerman, J.R. & Schultz, W. (1998). Dopamine neurons report an error in the temporal prediction of reward during learning. _Nature Neuroscience_, 1(4), 304–309.** 📄✅
`sources/papers/hollerman-schultz1998-reward-prediction-error.pdf` — Dopamine encodes
**reward prediction error**; unpredicted rewards drive stronger bursts; signal shifts to the
**anticipatory cue.** The mechanistic root of variable-reward compulsion. → note 02.

**Glimcher, P.W. (2011). Understanding dopamine and reinforcement learning: the dopamine reward prediction error hypothesis. _PNAS_ / PMC3176615.** 📄🌐 (open) → note 02.

## Remaining coverage gaps (lower priority — diminishing returns)

  evidence is mostly physical-activity/smoking/addiction; attention-specific trials are sparse).
- **Procrastination** research specifically (temporal motivation theory, Steel) — partially
  covered via present bias (note 06).
- **Notification-batching field experiments** (Fitz et al. "Batching smartphone notifications") — named, not yet pulled.
- Long-term (>6 mo) outcomes for **friction interventions** — One Sec is only 6 weeks.
